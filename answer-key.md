## Week 1: Classifying Matter

**W1-01** | S8P1.a | DOK 1 | mc | 1 pt

Which statement describes a pure substance?

- Answer: a) It has a fixed composition that is the same in every sample.
- Why: A pure substance (an element or a compound) has a fixed composition that does not change from sample to sample. Choice A describes a mixture. Choice D describes only an element; a compound is also a pure substance and has more than one kind of atom. Filtering separates mixtures, not pure substances.

**W1-02** | S8P1.a | DOK 1 | mc | 1 pt

Which of these is an element?

- Answer: d) Gold
- Why: Gold is made of only one kind of atom, so it is an element. Water and carbon dioxide are compounds (two kinds of atoms joined in a fixed ratio). Salt water is a mixture.

**W1-03** | S8P1.a | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

The model shows a sample of matter. Each ball is an atom. Which classification best describes the sample?

- Answer: a) A compound
- Why: Every particle is the same: two hydrogen atoms joined to one oxygen atom. One kind of particle with two kinds of atoms joined in a fixed ratio is a compound. It is not an element (two kinds of atoms) and not a mixture (only one kind of particle).

**W1-04** | S8P1.a | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

The model shows a sample of oxygen gas. Each ball is an atom. Which classification best describes the sample?

- Answer: c) An element
- Why: Each particle is two oxygen atoms joined. Only one kind of atom is present, so the sample is an element. Atoms that are joined do not automatically make a compound. A compound needs two or more different kinds of atoms.

**W1-05** | S8P1.a | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

The model shows a sample of gas. Each ball is a single atom. Which statement is correct?

- Answer: d) It is a mixture of two elements.
- Why: Helium atoms and neon atoms are present and are not joined to each other. Each keeps its own properties, so this is a mixture of two elements. Being evenly spread out does not make something a pure substance.

**W1-06** | S8P1.a | DOK 2 | ms | 1 pt

Which TWO samples are pure substances?

- Answer: a) Distilled water; c) Pure aluminum
- Why: Distilled water is a compound and pure aluminum is an element, so both are pure substances. Salt water, air, and trail mix each contain more than one substance, so they are mixtures.

**W1-07** | S8P1.a | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

A student stirs sugar into water until no sugar crystals can be seen. How should the result be classified?

- Answer: d) A homogeneous mixture, because the sugar and water are evenly mixed and each keeps its own properties
- Why: Sugar water is the same throughout, so it is homogeneous, and the sugar and water are only mixed, not joined. Looking the same throughout does not make something a pure substance. Heterogeneous means the parts are not uniform.

## Week 2: States of Matter and Particle Motion

**W2-01** | S8P1.b | DOK 1 | mc | 1 pt

Which statement describes the particles in a solid?

- Answer: c) They are close together and vibrate in fixed positions.
- Why: Particles in a solid are packed closely and held in place, but they still vibrate. Sliding past each other describes a liquid. Being far apart and moving freely describes a gas.

**W2-02** | S8P1.b | DOK 1 | mc | 1 pt

Which state of matter is made of electrically charged particles?

- Answer: a) Plasma
- Why: In plasma, particles have so much energy that electrons break away from atoms, leaving charged particles. A gas is not charged.

**W2-03** | S8P1.b | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

Models A, B, C, and D show the particles of one substance in four different states. Which model represents the liquid state?

- Answer: d) Model D
- Why: In a liquid the particles are close together but not in a fixed pattern, so they can slide past one another (Model D). Model A is a solid, Model B is a plasma (charged particles and free electrons), and Model C is a gas.

**W2-04** | S8P1.b | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

A sealed container holds a gas. The gas is cooled. What happens to the particles of the gas?

- Answer: a) They move more slowly because thermal energy transferred out of the gas.
- Why: Cooling means thermal energy leaves the gas, so the particles slow down. The particles do not change size and do not stop moving. Cold is not something that is added.

The models show the same gas in two identical sealed containers. The arrows show how fast the particles move. A longer arrow means a faster particle. Which statement correctly compares the gas in the two models?

- Answer: d) The gas in Model X has a higher temperature because its particles have more energy of motion.

**W2-07** | S8P1.b | DOK 2 | ms | 1 pt

As liquid water boils and changes to a gas, which TWO changes happen to the water particles?

- Answer: a) The particles move faster.; d) The particles move farther apart.

**W2-09** | S8P1.b | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

## Week 3: Physical and Chemical Properties and Changes

**W3-01** | S8P1.c | DOK 1 | mc | 1 pt

Which is a physical property of a substance?

- Answer: b) Melting point
- Why: A physical property can be observed or measured without changing the substance into a new one. Melting point is physical. Combustibility, reactivity, and rusting can only be observed when the substance reacts, so they are chemical properties.

**W3-02** | S8P1.c | DOK 1 | dnd | 1 pt

Move the labels into the table to show whether each property is a physical property or a chemical property.

- Answer: Density = physical property; Combustibility (the ability to burn in oxygen) = chemical property; Boiling point = physical property
- Why: Density and boiling point are measured without changing the substance (physical). Combustibility describes how the substance reacts with oxygen (chemical).

**W3-03** | S8P1.c | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

A teacher gives a student a clear, unlabeled liquid. The student measures a density of 0.79 g/mL and a boiling point of 65 °C. Use the table of known liquids to identify the unknown liquid.

- Answer: c) Methanol
- Why: Three liquids have a density of 0.79 g/mL, so density alone is not enough. Only methanol also has a boiling point of 65 °C. Using more than one property identifies the liquid.

**W3-04** | S8P1.c | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

A student measures two metal samples. Which statement is correct?

- Answer: d) Sample P has a greater density because the same mass is packed into a smaller volume.
- Why: Density is mass divided by volume. Sample P is 40 g in 10 mL (4 g/mL). Sample Q is 40 g in 20 mL (2 g/mL). The same mass in less space means a greater density.

**W3-05** | S8P1.c | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

A student measures the density of a small piece of aluminum (10 g) and a large piece of aluminum (50 g). Both pieces have a density of 2.7 g/mL. What does this show?

- Answer: a) Density is a property of the substance and does not depend on the amount of it.
- Why: Properties such as density are characteristic of the substance. A small piece and a large piece of the same material have the same density.

**W3-06** | S8P1.c | DOK 3 | mc | 1 pt

A student was given three unlabeled white powders and asked to write a procedure to help describe properties of the powders. The equipment available in the lab is shown. - Why: A chemical property is observed when a substance reacts. Adding vinegar and watching for a gas (bubbles) tests how each powder reacts. Dissolving, melting point, and crystal size are physical properties.

**W3-07** | S8P1.d | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

Which observation is the best evidence that a chemical change has occurred?

- Answer: c) Two clear liquids are mixed and a solid forms in the mixture.
- Why: A solid forming when two liquids are mixed (a precipitate) shows that a new substance formed. Melting, dissolving, and stretching change the form or arrangement but not the identity of the substance.

**W3-08** | S8P1.d | DOK 2 | ms | 1 pt

Which TWO changes are chemical changes?

- Answer: a) An iron nail rusts.; e) Wood burns in a campfire.
- Why: Rusting and burning make new substances with new properties. Freezing, dissolving, and crumpling only change the state, mixture, or shape.

**W3-11** | S8P1.d | DOK 3 | mp | 2 pt

**W3-12** | S8P1.f | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

**W3-15** | S8P1.f | DOK 3 | mp | 2 pt

- Answer: Part A: c) 8.8 g | Part B: c) The atoms in the iron and sulfur were rearranged into iron sulfide, so the total mass stayed the same.
- Why: Matter is conserved. The total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants: 5.6 g + 3.2 g = 8.8 g. The atoms are rearranged into a new substance.

**W4-01** | S8P1.f | DOK 1 | mc | 1 pt

In a chemical reaction, what are the starting substances called?

What happens to the atoms of the reactants in a chemical reaction?

- Answer: b) They are rearranged to form the products.
- Why: In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged into new combinations. Atoms are not destroyed, created, or changed into other elements.

- Answer: Least thermal energy = solid; Second = liquid; Third = gas; Most thermal energy = plasma
- Why: As thermal energy increases, particles move faster and spread out: solid, then liquid, then gas, then plasma.

- Answer: c) The liquid turns brown, gives off smoke, and smells burnt.
- Why: Melting is a physical change. A new color, smoke, and a new odor show that new substances formed, which is a chemical change.

- Answer: d) It is a physical change because the salt and water were only separated, and each kept its own properties.

**W5-13** | S8P1.f | DOK 3 | mc | 1 pt

A student burns a piece of steel wool (iron) in an open dish. The table shows the mass of the iron before burning and the mass of the solid left after burning. Which explanation best accounts for the increase in mass?

- Answer: c) Oxygen atoms from the air joined with the iron atoms, so the solid gained the mass of the oxygen.
- Why: The iron reacted with oxygen from the air. The oxygen atoms became part of the solid, so its mass increased by the mass of the oxygen. No matter was created. Atoms were rearranged.

**W5-14** | S8P1.f | DOK 2 | mc | 1 pt

When yeast is added to hydrogen peroxide, the hydrogen peroxide breaks down into water and oxygen gas. 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2 Which statement is supported by the equation?

- Answer: d) There are 4 hydrogen atoms and 4 oxygen atoms on each side of the equation.
- Why: Reactants: 2H2O2 has 4 H and 4 O. Products: 2H2O has 4 H and 2 O, and O2 adds 2 more O, for 4 H and 4 O. Both sides match, so matter is conserved.

Carbon monoxide reacts with oxygen. The model shows the reactants. Each ball is an atom.
